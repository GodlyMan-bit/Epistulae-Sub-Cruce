Whoever wishes to praise the Maker should praise marriage. It was instituted by the heavenly Father at creation, blessed by Christ, and made a sign of the union between Christ and His bride, the Church. Through Adam and Eve, the blessings of marriage filled the earth. Courage is derived from marriage, for without women and children to protect, what greater earthly cause would call men to defend their homes? Furthermore, marriage cultivates justice in men. A man must rule his household well. Matrimony is also wise, for it inspires men to care for their loved ones. In marriage, it is possible to enjoy the greatest delights through self-control and temperance, protecting a good thing from evil. So if marriage reflects Christ, and calls men to be courageous and just, wise and moderate, should it not be esteemed by all?

One might say, “Marriage is the leading cause of misfortune.” If you make this assertion, you are bringing a charge against fortune, not marriage. What men suffer in hard times is the product of fortune, not marriage; what marriage lavishes upon mankind is not mere chance. So marriage should be admired for the blessings it contains, not criticized for the evils which fortune dispenses.

And yet, even if marriage causes suffering at times, should we abandon it altogether? Just because the parts of a task may be unpleasant, that is no basis for abolishing it. Consider what patient tradesmen must suffer for their work. Thunderbolts and storms cause trouble for farmers, but farmers do not abandon their land because of one bad storm; they go on farming even when the torrents of rain descend from the heavens. Merchants may suffer at sea in order to garner its riches; they may endure ill fortune, but their love of the sea will endure. Likewise, warriors go into battle because they deem glory worth the risk of their lives. Men should not shun what is good because of what is bad. Therefore, if we approve farming, merchantry, and soldiery despite their hardships, we would lack sense if we condemned marriage for its arduous burdens.

“Does not marriage widow women and orphan children?” The suffering of the widow and the child is due to death, which afflicts all people. Remember that you are dust. This critique seems based on the assumption that marriage makes gods of men, making mortals immortal. Why condemn marriage for what death causes? Why should nuptial ties be blamed for mortality? Let death be attributed to the human condition, not to marital status. If a wife loses her husband, and children their father, due to human mortality, why would you blame marriage?

“Marriage is tiresome.” Is there anything besides marriage that can end tiresomeness? Marriage dispels weariness, and love is restful for every husband and wife. What joy for man and woman to unite! How pleasant to await a child in the womb! How delightful to watch that child grow, to teach him, to nourish him, and to see in him the continuation of one's own household. Even the most ordinary tasks become pleasant when performed for the sake of someone beloved. What might otherwise be a burden becomes an act of love. Indeed, is there any greater comfort in the troubles of this life than to have a faithful companion with whom to bear them?

“But does marriage not deprive a man of his freedom?” What sort of freedom is this that a man wishes to preserve? Is it the freedom to live without responsibility, to serve no one, and to concern himself with nothing beyond his own pleasures? Such freedom is hardly worth possessing. For man was not made to live unto himself alone. We are creatures of fellowship, called to love and serve our neighbors. Marriage gives a man a particular neighbor to whom he has promised his lifelong faithfulness. He is no longer free to live only for himself, but is bound in love to another. Yet what is this bondage but a good and willing service? The husband serves his wife, and the wife serves her husband. Each gives himself to the other, and in this giving both receive.

To credit marriage properly would require many pages. Furthermore, its goodness is so abundant that no single treatise could exhaust it. We have considered only a few of its blessings, and answered only a few of its critics. Yet what more need be said? That which God has instituted, let no man despise. That which He has called good, let no man call evil.

Let us therefore praise marriage, not because it is without hardship, but because it is good; not because it guarantees happiness, but because it is a gift from the Creator. Let husbands cherish their wives, and wives their husbands. Let children be received as blessings, and households be ordered in love and faithfulness. Above all, let us remember the greater mystery to which marriage bears witness: Christ and the Church. For earthly marriage, though it be a great blessing, is temporal; but the love of Christ for His bride is everlasting.

Therefore, **should you marry?** Esteem marriage, whether or not you enter it. And if you are called to this vocation, receive it with thanksgiving, undertake it with solemnity, and remain faithful in love. For whoever wishes to praise the Maker should praise His works, and among the blessings of creation, marriage is surely one of the greatest.
